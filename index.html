Hi! I'm Helen, and I'm a 3rd-year PhD student working in Computational Neuroscience.

My overall research direction is to understand how the human brain and foundation models process and generate continuous streams of information. Specifically, I leverage what we know about the brain and GenAI models to study and decode human neural activity. I primarily work with neuroimaging data and have extensive experience working with magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the brain.

Here is my recent publication on using Hidden Markov Models to decode human brain activity related to event parsing while participants listened to two auditory stories. In the past, I have also worked on a project decoding human emotional states from fMRI data collected while participants watched two short animations designed to evoke mixed feelings.

Recently, I have been investigating (1) which brain regions' activity can be best predicted by LLM embeddings during information processing (Angular Gyrus seems to be a winner!), and (2) the dissociation between regions involved in thinking about oneself vs. thinking about others within the medial default mode network. The default mode network (DMN) is a set of interconnected brain regions that are most active during internally focused processes such as self-referential thinking, imagining others’ mental states, and processing continuous information.

Outside the field of neuroscience, I have a longstanding interest in mathematics and statistics.
